A fitness watch (or fitness tracker) is a wearable device—usually worn on the wrist—designed to monitor and collect data on physical activity, heart health, and sleep patterns. These devices use sensors like accelerometers and optical heart rate monitors to track steps, distance, calories burned, and exercise intensity, sending this data to a paired smartphone app for analysis. Many fitness trackers are similar to pedometers, but in addition to counting steps they contain additional sensors such as accelerometers and altimeters to collect or estimate fitness and exercise information, including the speed and distance travelled, heart rate, calorie expenditure, or the duration and quality of sleep.

Apple Watch

The Apple Watch is a premier line of smartwatches developed by Apple Inc., designed for fitness tracking, health monitoring, and wireless communication, running on watchOS. It integrates deeply with iPhones to manage notifications, apps, and calls, while offering advanced health features like ECG, heart rate monitoring, and sleep tracking.

Samsung Watch

The Samsung Galaxy Watch series consists of premium, Wear OS-powered smartwatches designed for health tracking, fitness, and phone integration. Key models (Watch6, Watch7, Ultra) feature durable Sapphire Crystal displays, aluminum or titanium frames, advanced BioActive sensors for wellness monitoring, and AI-powered health insights.
